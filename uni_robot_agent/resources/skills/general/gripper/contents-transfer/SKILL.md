# Observe where the contents actually go

Use `general/SKILL.md` for holding and transport. Identify the requested source,
recipient and transfer amount from the current instruction. Keep source-to-target
pairings explicit when several similar containers are present.

Preserve contents while establishing a secure grasp and reachable route. An
exploratory tip can spill before the receiving opening is ready. Choose contact
from the vessel's current support and orientation; an upright approach does not
transfer unchanged to a vessel lying on its side.

Observe the actual outlet and receiving opening. Establish their relationship
before tilting, then change tilt in bounded segments and inspect the flow or
falling contents. If they miss, stop increasing tilt and correct the outlet-to-
opening relationship while preserving the remaining contents. Rotation about the grasp site
alone does not establish transfer, and releasing the vessel does not replace
pouring its contents.

Inspect the receiver, remaining source contents and visible spills together.
An empty source alone does not prove delivery to the intended destination.
When flow is correctly directed in a step-driven environment, use hold to advance
physics and observe between intervals. Job-wait tools wait for submitted work;
read-only observations do not themselves advance a stepped transfer. In a
continuously running environment, account for elapsed time and observation latency.

For full transfer, first arrival in the receiver is insufficient. If visible
contents remain after flow stops, inspect the outlet and adjust tilt or position
as needed. For a specified partial transfer, judge progress against that amount;
do not apply an empty-source rule. Keep an unobservable amount uncertain.

Return the source to stable support while retaining the load and remaining
contents. After a spill, inspect what remains feasible before recovery; gathering
individual loose items need not be equivalent to the instructed operation.
